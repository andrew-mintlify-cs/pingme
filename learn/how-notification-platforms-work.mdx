You don't need to write code to understand PingMe. This guide explains the moving parts using the same vocabulary you'll see in the rest of the docs.

## The problem PingMe solves

Every product eventually needs to tell its users things: *your account is ready*, *your code is 482913*, *your trial ends in 3 days*, *someone commented on your document*. At first that's a single "send email" function. Then someone asks for SMS. Then mobile push. Then a user complains they got the same alert three times on three channels. Then legal asks about unsubscribe handling.

Most engineering teams end up building a small notifications system by accident. PingMe is that system, built once and maintained for you.

## The five moving parts

<Steps>
  <Step title="Recipients">
    A **recipient** is a person you might send to. PingMe stores their contact details (email, phone, device tokens), their locale and time zone, and their **preferences** — which channels they've agreed to receive. You usually create a recipient when a user signs up for your product.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Channels">
    A **channel** is a way to reach someone: email, SMS, push, in-app, or Slack. Each channel has its own strengths. SMS is immediate but expensive; email is cheap but slow; push is free but only works if the app is installed. A good notification uses the channel the message deserves.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Templates">
    A **template** is reusable message content with blanks in it, like `Welcome to {{workspace}}, {{first_name}}!` You fill the blanks when you send. Templates are versioned, so changing the wording never breaks the code that sends them.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Messages">
    A **message** is one send to one recipient on one or more channels. Your app says *send template X to recipient Y on email and push*, and PingMe does the rest — including skipping channels the recipient has opted out of.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Deliveries and events">
    Behind each message is one **delivery** per channel with its own status (queued, sent, delivered, failed). PingMe reports these back through **webhooks** — automatic notifications to *your* servers — so your product can react, for example by showing "email sent" in your UI.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What happens when a message is sent

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="The happy path">
    1. Your app calls PingMe with a recipient, a template, and a list of channels.
    2. PingMe checks the recipient's preferences and quiet hours and drops any channel they've opted out of.
    3. PingMe renders the template with the values you supplied.
    4. Each remaining channel is handed to the right provider — an email gateway, a carrier, Apple or Google's push service, Slack.
    5. As providers confirm delivery, PingMe updates each delivery record and sends `message.delivered` events to your webhook endpoint.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="When something fails">
    1. A provider rejects a send (an invalid device token, a full mailbox, a disconnected phone number).
    2. PingMe retries transient errors automatically with increasing delays.
    3. If the channel still fails, that delivery is marked `failed` with the provider's reason.
    4. PingMe sends a `message.failed` event so your app can fall back — for example, sending an email when push fails.
    5. The message's overall status becomes `partially_delivered` (some channels worked) or `failed` (none did).
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="When a user opts out">
    1. A user clicks an unsubscribe link, replies STOP to an SMS, or flips a toggle in your settings page.
    2. PingMe records the preference on the recipient.
    3. Future sends on that channel are silently **skipped**, not failed — so your code doesn't need special handling.
    4. PingMe sends a `recipient.unsubscribed` event so you can mirror the change in your own database.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Build or buy?

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Build it yourself when…" icon="hammer">
    You send one channel, at low volume, to users who never need to change preferences — and you're confident that will stay true.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Use a platform when…" icon="rocket">
    You send on two or more channels, need unsubscribe compliance, want delivery visibility, or your team would rather ship product features than maintain retry logic.
  </Card>
</Columns>
